## Where to get the link

The subscription link comes from your VPN service — usually in your account dashboard, a Telegram bot
or an email after payment. It looks like this:

```
https://sub.example.com/a1b2c3d4
```

Only `https://` addresses are accepted — otherwise the subscription address would travel over the
network in plain text.

<Callout type="warn" title="The link is a key">
Anyone with the link can use your subscription. Don't publish it or forward it to strangers.
</Callout>

## The first subscription

On first launch the home screen shows the **"Add your subscription"** field. Paste the link and press
**Add**.

<Shot src="ru/welcome.png" alt="First launch: the “Add your subscription” field" />

The app first checks the provider's secure channel and shows on screen what is happening, then
downloads the subscription. The name, expiry date, traffic and server list come with the link —
there's nothing else to enter. You can connect right after that.

## Another subscription

Further subscriptions are added in advanced mode: the **Subscriptions** tile → **Add subscription**.
Paste the link into the **Subscription URL** field and press **Add**.

<Shot src="ru/add-subscription.png" alt="The “Add subscription” window" width={720} />

You only need the **Advanced** block if you want to override something: your own name, group,
update interval, User-Agent. **Your own config file** adds a subscription from a file on your
computer instead of a link.

### Invitation link

If your provider gave you a link like `clash://install-config?url=https://…` or
`clodclash://install-config?url=https://…`, just open it — the app picks up the subscription by
itself, even when minimized to the tray. The address inside the link must also be `https://`.

### Secure connection

This is not ordinary HTTPS (which is always used) but additional encryption of the exchange with the
provider on top of it: the subscription address, device data and the server list travel to the
provider encrypted, and intermediaries along the way (CDNs, proxies) can't see them. It works if the
provider supports the channel.

There's no switch when adding — the app decides everything by itself:

- the provider has the channel — the subscription is added through it;
- there's no channel — the app downloads the subscription the usual way and says it was added
  without the secure channel;
- the server doesn't respond — the app retries over the secure channel, three times in total, and if
  there's still no answer, it shows "The provider's server is not responding"; the subscription is
  not added.

In the subscription properties you can turn the channel off — the app will warn that this is
unsafe — and turn it back on: when turning it on, the app first checks whether the channel responds,
and if it doesn't, the channel stays off.

## Several subscriptions

You can have as many subscriptions as you like; only one is in use — the one with a frame around
it. To switch, tap another card on the **Subscriptions** screen.

<Shot src="ru/profiles.png" alt="The Subscriptions screen: three subscriptions, the selected one is framed" width={720} />

The card shows the subscription's domain, when it was updated, traffic usage and how many days are
left. Badges:

| Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Active** | the subscription your traffic is currently going through |
| **Expiring** (yellow) | 3 days or fewer left until the end of the term |
| **Expired** (red) | the term is over; an inactive card is dimmed |
| **Out of traffic** (red) | the term is still valid, but the limit is used up |
| **not applied** | the subscription was downloaded, but the core didn't accept it — the previous config is in use |

If an auto-update fails, the card shows "Not updated · updated …", and if it fails several times in
a row — an **"Update the subscription"** frame.

### Subscription menu

Right-clicking a card opens the menu.

<Shot src="ru/profile-menu.png" alt="Subscription menu" width={720} />

- **Subscription page** — the provider's page, if it sent one;
- **Select** — make this the subscription in use;
- **Update** and **Update via proxy** — download the subscription again;
- **Advanced** — Edit Info, Edit File, Edit Rules, Edit Proxies and Edit Proxy Groups, Open File,
  Share QR Code, Delete.

### Subscription properties

**Advanced → Edit Info**.

<Shot src="ru/profile-edit.png" alt="Subscription properties" width={720} />

- **Name (optional)** — your own name for the subscription. It's stored separately from the name the
  panel sends and isn't overwritten by updates; it's shown as "Yours (Name from the panel)".
- **Group** — for convenience: a row of group filters appears above the cards. It doesn't affect
  how anything works.
- **Update Interval** — in minutes, no less than 60. If the provider sent an interval when the
  subscription was added, the field is locked.
- **Allow Auto Update**, **Secure connection**, updating through a proxy, User-Agent and the request
  timeout.

If you changed the subscription address (for example, reissued the link), the selected mode and
servers are kept.

## Updating the subscription

The subscription is updated at the interval the provider sends — a new value is applied with every
update until you set your own. A missed update runs at launch; the timer keeps running even when the
window is closed.

Without an interval the subscription is updated manually — and also on its own, a minute and a half
after the term ends, to show the renewal or the "expired" state right away (unchecking "Allow Auto
Update" prevents this).

Manually:

- the ↻ button next to the subscription name on the home screen;
- in advanced mode — the **Refresh subscription** tile (its tooltip shows when the last update
  happened);
- the ↻ button on a card on the **Subscriptions** screen, or the ↻ "Update All Profiles" button in its header.

After a manual update you can repeat it in half a minute.

If the provider's domain is blocked, the download goes down a ladder: directly → through the app's
own tunnel → through the system proxy. So you can update the subscription even when the provider's
website doesn't open.

A failed update doesn't take away a working subscription: if the panel responded with something other
than a config or the core didn't accept it, the previous file stays, the card is marked "not
applied", and the reason is shown in a notification. Updating the subscription doesn't drop live
connections or reset the selected server.

## Traffic and expiry

The **Traffic** and **Expires** cards on the home screen get their data from the panel on every
update. The last day of the term is shown in hours, an unlimited plan in words. Usage between
updates is calculated locally and marked as approximate.

The app reminds you about the end of the term 7, 3 and 1 day ahead and about traffic usage at 80,
90 and 100% — the provider can set its own thresholds. All of them are turned off at once in
**Settings → Advanced settings → Appearance and behaviour → Subscription notifications**.

## Backup address

Along with the subscription, the provider can send a **backup address** — the same subscription on a
different domain. It's tried only if no usable subscription could be fetched from the main address by any route
(directly, through the core, through the system proxy), and it doesn't change the saved link. If the provider moves everyone to the backup address, the app first checks that it really
serves the subscription, then replaces the link and lets you know.

## Device limit

If your plan has a device limit, the panel recognizes the computer by its device identifier. It's
derived from the system's machine ID, hashed, and doesn't change between app updates; the machine ID
itself and the computer name are never sent anywhere (more in
[Support report](/en/docs/user/support#device-identifier)).

When the limit is reached, the app shows a "Device limit reached" window with the subscription name
and a **Support** button — both when adding such a subscription and on every update of it. With
such a response, the subscription's previous servers are removed, and the card shows the line "Not
updating: the device limit is full". After a renewal or once a slot is freed, the very next update
brings the servers back.

You can turn off sending the identifier in **Settings → General → Device identification** — but a
panel with a device limit will then refuse, and the app will offer to turn identification back on.
